HLEND is gated, and it's the only fund that says by how much

Redemption requests were 9.25%, 13.30% and 11.44% of shares in Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026, against a 5% quarterly offer. In Q3, investors got about 44% of what they asked for. In 2025, every request was met in full.

Fees on one basis: leverage and performance fees drive the cost gap

Fund fee tables aren't directly comparable: some include interest on borrowings, some include performance fees, and K-PEC's management fee was offset by credits in FY2025. I split annual cost into three layers and used one definition of base fees for all four.

  • Base fees = management fee (1.25% for all four) + Class I servicing fee (0%) + other operating expenses.
  • K-PEC shown at its contractual fee. In FY2025 its $78.7M management fee was offset by $80.8M of fee credits.
  • K-PEC and BXPE are FY2025 actuals; BCRED and HLEND are N-2 prospectus estimates.
  • Interest on borrowings is a cost of leverage that also raises income, not a fee paid to the manager.

The recommended mix is the only one that passes both client limits

Model client

  • Accredited high-net-worth investor, $3,000,000 liquid portfolio, 60/40 stocks/bonds
  • 15% alternatives sleeve: $450,000; income plus growth; horizon 7+ years
  • Limited tolerance for gated redemptions; fee-conscious

I made the gating constraint testable: at most 40% of the sleeve in funds prorating today, and at most 70% in any one fund. Both limits are my assumptions and are editable inputs in the workbook.

Why this mix

  • K-PEC, 65% ($292,500): the only eligible fund that isn't prorating, and it is still taking in money.
  • HLEND, 35% ($157,500): gated, but the stronger credit book (0.8% non-accruals at cost, 94.6% first lien) and it provides the income.
  • BCRED, 0%: three flags at once. Back on the list if non-accruals fall below 2%, proration ends and flows turn positive.
  • BXPE, 0%: screened out on eligibility. It's open only to qualified purchasers ($5M+), and the client has $3M. Its 22.4% 1-year return was the best of the four, which is why I check eligibility before returns.

Trade-offs accepted: less income from the sleeve (bonds stay the main income source), and 65% of the sleeve in one KKR vehicle, 9.75% of the whole portfolio.

Risks I'm accepting

  • Gating: 35% of the sleeve sits in a fund that is prorating today.
  • Concentration: 65% in K-PEC.
  • Valuation lag: PE NAVs use manager marks and may lag public markets.
  • Income shortfall: 3.4% blended yield, and credit distributions are falling as rates fall.
  • Rate sensitivity: about 99% of credit fund loans are floating rate.
  • Early exit cost: 5% deduction on K-PEC shares held under 2 years.

What triggers a review each quarter

  • Non-accruals above 2% at cost, or rising two quarters in a row
  • Debt-to-equity above 1.25x
  • Proration lasting four quarters
  • Distributions down more than 10% year over year
  • A PE fund using more than half its repurchase limit

Method, sources and data gaps

  • 55 SEC filings from EDGAR: 38 10-Qs, 15 10-Ks and 2 N-2 prospectuses (K-PEC 12, BXPE 12, BCRED 16, HLEND 15).
  • One common date: 9/30/2026, Class I shares. 12 quarters of NAV history and 11 quarters of flows.
  • Excel workbook built with Python (openpyxl): 10 tabs, live formulas, a source citation next to every input, conditional-format risk flags and native charts. Recalculated with zero formula errors.
  • Total returns chain-linked from fund-reported period returns.
  • Passed on: Apollo Aligned Alternatives (no 10-Q or 10-K on EDGAR); Hamilton Lane and StepStone registered funds (latest full data six months older).

USD.AI: lend to it through its loan exchange, and wait on the token

USD.AI (built by Permian Labs) is an onchain lender that turns stablecoin deposits into GPU loans for small and mid-sized AI cloud operators, the "compute middle market" that banks and large private credit funds mostly skip. My 12-page memo recommends a $5M to $10M lending pilot in its seasoned GPU loans, spread across at least three borrowers, and passing on the CHIP token until loan performance and deposit stability are proven.

How the money moves

  1. DepositStablecoins swapped for USDai, backed 1:1 by PYUSD. Earns nothing.
  2. StakeUSDai staked into sUSDai, a share of the lending vault. Exits through a monthly queue paid from available cash.
  3. LendNon-recourse loans at 70% to 80% LTV to a company that owns the borrower's GPUs; 1.15x DSCR cash sweep.
  4. Season and sellCash waits in Wilmington Trust escrow until GPUs are installed; within about a year the loan is sold through GLX.
  5. RecycleSale proceeds go back into the vault to fund the next loan.

Recommendation

  • Later: revisit a token or equity position only if all four hold: two more quarters with no losses, a redemption cycle without a large outflow, sUSDai yield at or above 7% without top-ups, and the largest borrower under 25% of the book.
